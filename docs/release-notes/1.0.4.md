## New in 1.0.4

### Match Minerals now matches several peaks at once

Until now, Match Minerals scored the reference minerals against **one peak** —
the one selected in the Peaks window. With a single peak a mineral can only be
judged on how close one of its lines sits; the intensities never come into it,
and on a kaolinite peak a zeolite could rank above kaolinite.

- **Targets**, at the top of the dialog, lists every peak with a tick box. Start
  from the peak you selected, then tick the other peaks you think belong to the
  same mineral. The scores update after every tick, and the list stays open so
  you can tick several. On the example project, ticking kaolinite's other two
  peaks takes kaolinite from second place to a clear first.
- **The target peaks are highlighted** on the plot, so you can see which peaks
  are being matched while you look at the reference lines.
- **Append labels** writes the chosen mineral onto every target peak at once.
- **Pairing** chooses how a mineral's reference lines are paired with your
  peaks. *Greedy* is how MudLab has always done it, and stays the default.
  *One-to-one* finds the pairing that matches the most lines; it is fairer to
  minerals with several lines close together.

While the dialog is open the plot can be **zoomed and panned but not clicked**.
Before, double-clicking any peak on the plot closed Match Minerals and threw
away its matches.

### Build a clay component from a published structure (CIF)

**Edit Phases → Components tab → Import CIF…** reads a crystal structure file
and turns it into a MudLab component, projecting the structure onto the c\*
axis. A review window shows every decision the projection had to make — how
many layers the cell holds, which oxygens are hydroxyls or water, which atoms
belong to the layer, the basal spacing, and whether the clay is 1:1 or 2:1 —
and each can be corrected. Nothing is replaced until you press OK.

A published structure is one clay in one state. **Right-click a phase → Create
treatment states…** builds its glycolated (**-EG**) and heated (**-350**)
partners. They share the original's layer, so refining the layer refines the
whole series.

### How MudLab Works

**Help → How MudLab Works** (Shift+F1) opens a new reference on the science
behind the program, in six parts: what a clay is to MudLab, from atoms to a
layer, stacking, from a layer to a pattern, fitting, and identification and
chemistry. It sits beside the manual in the same viewer.

The viewer can now **print** any page (the print dialog's *Print to PDF* gives
you a PDF) and **export** it as an Open Document text file — with real headings,
lists and tables, opening cleanly in Word and LibreOffice — or as a web page,
Markdown or plain text.

### Also

- The documentation said a mineral's *strongest* lines are matched. It is the
  fifteen lines at the largest d-spacings, and the text now says so.
